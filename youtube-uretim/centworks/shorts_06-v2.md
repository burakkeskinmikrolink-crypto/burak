## Short 1: Kim ödüyor? (~34 sn)

| Zaman | Görsel | Ekran yazısı |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 sn | **Hareketli:** "0%" butonu basılıyor ve parçalara ayrılıyor | ZERO INTEREST. SO WHO PAYS? |
| 2–12 sn | BNPL şirketi mağazaya peşin ödüyor, eksi bir ücret | 1. THE STORE PAYS A FEE |
| 12–20 sn | Kırmızı takvim günü | 2. SOME CHARGE LATE FEES |
| 20–30 sn | "6–24 months" etiketi | 3. LONGER PLANS: INTEREST |
| 30–34 sn | Butona geri dönüş | — |

**VO:**
> Zero interest. So who pays?
> One: the store. The buy-now-pay-later company pays the merchant up front, minus a fee, because "four easy payments" makes people buy more.
> Two: some providers charge late fees if a payment fails. Three: longer plans, six to twenty-four months, often charge interest. It's a family of products, and only some are free. So before you tap that button this weekend, ask:

**Döngü:** "…ask:" → "Zero interest. So who pays?"

| 0–2 sn | **Hareketli:** "4.1%" ve "47%" çarpışarak ekrana geliyor | 4.1% OR 47%? BOTH TRUE. |
| 2–12 sn | "4.1%" + "CFPB · loans · 2023" | LOANS WITH A LATE FEE |
| 12–20 sn | "47%" + "Survey · people · 2026" | USERS WHO PAID LATE |
| 20–34 sn | Bir kişi, altı kredi ikonu, biri kırmızı | 6 LOANS, 1 LATE |

**VO:**
> 4.1% or 47%? Both true.
> The CFPB found four point one percent of pay-in-four loans got a late fee in 2023. A 2026 LendingTree survey found forty-seven percent of users said they'd paid late in the past year.
> One counts loans. The other counts people. Take out six loans, pay one late, and most loans were fine — but you still paid late. Which is why the honest answer to "how often do people pay late" is:

**Döngü:** "…is:" → "4.1% or 47%? Both true."

## Short 3: Tek soru (Cyber Monday POV, ~30 sn)

| Zaman | Görsel | Ekran yazısı |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 sn | **Hareketli:** POV: parmak iki buton arasında gidip geliyor | CAN YOU PAY IT TODAY? |
| 2–12 sn | "YES" → kart, "paid in full" | YES → CARD, PAID IN FULL |
| 12–20 sn | "A FEW WEEKS" → tek plan | ONE PLAN, ON TIME |
| 20–30 sn | "NO" → sepetten ürün çıkıyor | NO → NEITHER BUTTON HELPS |

**VO:**
> Can you pay it today?
> If yes, a credit card paid in full usually wins: no interest, purchase protection, maybe rewards. If you need a few weeks, one pay-in-four plan, paid on time, can cost nothing.
> And if you stack plans across apps, every due date is yours to track. If no, neither button makes it affordable. They just change when the bill arrives. So this Cyber Monday, before you tap anything, one question:

**Döngü:** "…one question:" → "Can you pay it today?"
