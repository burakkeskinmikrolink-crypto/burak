| 2–9 sn | Şıklar | A) 6 YRS B) 12 YRS C) 20 YRS |

| 12–24 sn | "72 ÷ 6 = 12" | B) 72 ÷ 6 = 12 |

**VO:**
> Double your money at 6%?
> How long does it take? A: six years. B: twelve. C: twenty. It's B. Use the rule of 72: divide 72 by the yearly rate. Seventy-two divided by six is twelve. It works in reverse, too. At 3% inflation, prices double in about 24 years. And an unpaid balance at 24% interest can double in about three. It's an estimate, not a promise. But it shows fast whether time is on your side. So, how long to…

> Same price. Less in the bag.
> That's shrinkflation, and the government's inflation numbers do count it. The Bureau of Labor Statistics converts prices to a price per unit, like per ounce. So when a bag shrinks from 16 ounces to 14 at the same price, the inflation index records a price increase. It isn't always easy to catch. In normal years, BLS found size changes added only a tiny amount to overall inflation. But at the grocery store, you notice, because it's the…

**VO:**
> $2.99 or $3.00?
> One cent apart. But many shoppers judge the first as noticeably cheaper. Researchers call it the left-digit effect: we read prices left to right, and the first digit drops from three to two. A 2005 study in the Journal of Consumer Research found the effect is strongest exactly when that left digit changes. That's why so many Black Friday tags end in nine. A simple fix: round every price up in your head. Then ask again:
