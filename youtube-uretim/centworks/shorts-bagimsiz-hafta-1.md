> How long does $1 last?
> A: about two years. B: about six and a half. C: about twenty. It's B. The Treasury estimates the average one dollar note lasts about 6.6 years before it's worn out. The hundred lasts about 22.9 years. Why? Small bills change hands all day. Big bills mostly sit in safes and wallets. When a worn note reaches a Federal Reserve bank, machines spot it and shred it. **VO:**
> Why do quarters have ridges?
> Centuries ago, coins were made of real silver and gold, and people shaved metal off the edges to keep. Ridged edges, called reeding, made that shaving obvious. U.S. dimes, quarters and half dollars kept the ridges long after they stopped being silver. Pennies and nickels never had them. They were never worth shaving.
