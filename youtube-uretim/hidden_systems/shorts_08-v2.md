**VO:**
> Why cut power on purpose?
> Because a grid has to match supply and demand every second. When demand outruns supply, frequency falls, and power stations start disconnecting to protect themselves, which makes the shortfall worse. That's how a shortage becomes a collapse. So operators cut some customers, in planned blocks, and rotate them, so the whole system survives. On Christmas Eve 2022, that's exactly what happened in the eastern United States. > 90,500 megawatts gone at once.
> That's how much generation was unavailable at the worst moment of Winter Storm Elliott, in December 2022. Roughly thirteen percent of the eastern US grid's resources. Investigators found that of the units that froze, nearly eighty percent failed in temperatures they were supposed to handle. And the gas that fuels many of them froze at the wells. | 12–22 sn | "Nearly 4,000 homes: over a week" | SOME WAITED OVER A WEEK |
| 22–33 sn | Masada fener, powerbank, not kâğıdı "105" | TORCH. POWER BANK. 105. |

**VO:**
> Power cut? Dial 105.
> In Great Britain, 105 is the free number for reporting or checking a power cut, whichever company supplies you. In November 2021, Storm Arwen brought down lines across the north, and nearly four thousand customers were without power for over a week. Britain's winter blackouts usually come from damage, not shortage. So before the next storm: a torch, a charged power bank, and one number to remember. Lights go out?
