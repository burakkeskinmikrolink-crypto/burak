**VO:**
> Britain runs at 50 hertz.
> Every big generator in the country spins in step with the grid, fifty cycles a second. The rules allow the frequency to drift only between 49.5 and 50.5. If it falls much further, automatic systems start disconnecting customers to save everyone else. So the control room watches one number, every second of every day, because supply and demand must match, or that number moves. Right now, as you watch this, Britain runs…

They study the TV schedule and keep fast power ready, like hydro stations that start in seconds. Because the grid still remembers…

| Zaman | Görsel | Ekran yazısı |

| 0–2 sn | **Hareketli:** Dört peynir dilimi kayarak üst üste diziliyor, bir ok deliklerden geçiyor | EVERY DISASTER HAS LINED-UP HOLES. |
| 2–12 sn | Dilimlere etiket: BACKUP, ALARM, CHECKLIST, PERSON | EACH SLICE IS A DEFENCE |
| 12–24 sn | Delikler kayıyor, bir an hizalanıyor | THE HOLES MOVE |
| 24–33 sn | Kaza raporu sayfaları (kendi grafik) | NOT ONE CAUSE. A STACK. |

**VO:**
> Every disaster has lined-up holes.
> In 2000, the psychologist James Reason described accidents with a simple picture: slices of Swiss cheese. Each slice is a defence: a backup, an alarm, a checklist, a person. Every slice has holes, and the holes keep moving. Most days, one slice catches what another misses. A disaster happens when, for one moment, the holes in every slice line up. That's why investigators rarely find one cause. They find a stack. Because every disaster…

**Döngü:** "…every disaster…" → "Every disaster has lined-up holes."

> One typo took down the internet?

Even Amazon's status page couldn't update, because it relied on S3 too. So, did one typo…
