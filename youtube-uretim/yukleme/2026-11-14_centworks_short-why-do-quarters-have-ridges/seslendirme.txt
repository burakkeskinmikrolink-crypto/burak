Why do quarters have ridges? [pause 400]
Centuries ago, coins were made of real silver and gold, and people shaved metal off the edges to keep. Ridged edges, called reeding, made that shaving obvious. U.S. dimes, quarters and half dollars kept the ridges long after they stopped being silver. Pennies and nickels never had them. They were never worth shaving.
