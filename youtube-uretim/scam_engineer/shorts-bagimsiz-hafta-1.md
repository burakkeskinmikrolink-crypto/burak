## Short 1 — 11 Kas | QUIZ: "IRS" arıyor (~33 sn)

| Zaman | Görsel | Ekran yazısı |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 sn | **Hareketli:** Telefon titriyor, ekranda kurgusal "IRS – Collections" yazıyor | THE IRS CALLS. PAY HOW? |
| 2–12 sn | Şıklar tek tek düşüyor | A) GIFT CARDS B) A WIRE C) NEITHER |
| 12–15 sn | Sayaç | 3… 2… 1… |
| 15–33 sn | "C" yeşil; zarf simgesi "MAIL FIRST" | C) THE IRS WRITES FIRST |

**VO:**
> The IRS calls. Pay how?
> A: gift cards, today. B: a wire transfer, within the hour. C: neither, because the real IRS doesn't work that way. It's C. The IRS normally contacts you first by mail. It doesn't demand immediate payment by gift card, and it doesn't threaten arrest over the phone. If you're not sure whether you owe anything, find the number on IRS.gov and call it yourself. So next time your phone says…

**Döngü:** "…your phone says…" → "The IRS calls. Pay how?"

## Short 2 — 13 Kas | KISA HİKÂYE: Bebek arabası satışı (~36 sn)

| Zaman | Görsel | Ekran yazısı |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 sn | **Hareketli:** İkinci el ilan uygulaması (kurgusal, logosuz), "Stroller – $80" kartı kayarak giriyor. Köşede "Composite character" | SHE WAS SELLING A STROLLER. |
| 2–14 sn | Mesaj balonu: "I need to make sure you're real. Read me the code." | "JUST READ ME THE CODE" |
| 14–26 sn | 6 haneli kod bildirimi, üstünde anahtar simgesi | THE CODE WAS A KEY |
| 26–36 sn | Büyük yazı: "Codes are for you only" | NEVER SHARE A CODE |

**VO:**
> She was selling a stroller.
> A buyer messaged Ann: "Before I drive over, I need to make sure you're real. I'm sending you a code. Just read it back." A six-digit code arrived. It didn't prove anything. It let the scammer set up a Google Voice number linked to Ann's phone number, to use in other scams. The FTC puts it simply: if someone you didn't contact asks for a verification code, it's a scam, every time. And it began so simply. She was just…

**Döngü:** "…She was just…" → "She was selling a stroller."

## Short 3 — 14 Kas | GERÇEK Mİ SAHTE Mİ?: Kargo mesajı (~33 sn)

| Zaman | Görsel | Ekran yazısı |
|---|---|---|
| 0–2 sn | **Hareketli:** Üç SMS balonu sırayla patlayarak açılıyor (kurgusal metin, gerçek link yok) | REAL OR FAKE? A PACKAGE TEXT. |
| 2–12 sn | "Your package is on hold. Update your address: [link]" | FAKE |
| 12–20 sn | "Tracking update you requested. No link to pay." | CAN BE REAL |
| 20–33 sn | "Redelivery fee $0.30: [link]" | FAKE |

**VO:**
> Real or fake? A package text.
> "Your package is on hold. Update your address here," with a link. Fake. The Postal Inspection Service says USPS won't text you a link unless you signed up for tracking on that package. Second: a tracking update you asked for, with nothing to pay. Can be real. Third: a tiny redelivery fee, by link. Fake. Not sure? Type the website yourself and enter the tracking number. Next text you get, ask: real or fake…

**Döngü:** "…real or fake…" → "Real or fake? A package text."
**Kaynak:** U.S. Postal Inspection Service – Smishing; FTC – New FTC data show top text message scams of 2024

The FCC warns about "one ring" calls: robocalls that hang up after a ring, hoping you'll call back an international number that bills like a premium line. If someone really needs you, they'll call again or leave a message. Your carrier can also block international calls. So, next time it's…

**Döngü:** "…next time it's…" → "One ring, then silence. Call back?"
